How right were the odds? Each week's odds, as they stood at the lineup lock, against what happened. A single week is mostly luck; the picture sharpens as the weeks add up.

How it's worked out

Favourites. Each matchup's (or category's) favourite is the team the odds gave the better chance. Its expected result is its chance of winning plus half its chance of a tie; it gets 1 for a win, ½ for a tie, 0 for a loss. Luck alone moves the total a little either way; the green band is the range luck explains (two standard deviations). Outside it, the odds were too sure (favourites lost more often than they said) or too timid.

Better than a coin flip. Every forecast is scored by the ranked probability score: how far its win, tie and loss chances were from what happened (a tie counts as close to both a win and a loss). "Beats a coin flip by 30%" means its score was 30% better than calling every one 50:50. Perfect odds can't reach 100%: games are random. The black tick is what the odds would reach if they were exactly right, so a bar short of its tick means the odds claimed more than they delivered.

Team totals. For every team, week and category: how far the actual total was from the projection, in units of the projection's own uncertainty (from each player's game-to-game variation). If the projections were right, these would follow the grey bell curve.

Luck. Each team's matchup results (a win 1, a tie ½) minus what the odds expected of it, added up over the weeks.

A week with two lineup locks (7, 17) is graded on each half as predicted at its own lock.
